My research focuses on how autonomous systems can better understand and adapt to the people they are working with.

Projects

  • More recently my work has shifted to lower limb exoskeleton systems. Here I am developing control strategies that consider how people's needs change with fatigue. Stay tuned for updates!

    • Semi-Autonomous Vehicles

  • Adaptive Training Paradigm

    I developed a paradigm to better prepare drivers to take over an autonomous vehicle in the presence of an obstacle that the autonomy fails to perceive. We iterated on various ways to estimate model parameters online, as seen in this abstract. I conducted a user study where I was able to show a 20% reduction in take over variability between users that completed the adaptive training versus the baseline protocol. This work has been submitted to IEEE Transactions on Human-Machine Systems.

  • Game-Theoretic Modeling of Human-Autonomy Teaming

    My initial work sought to develop a theoretically grounded method to characterize human behavior (i.e., cognitive, high-level behavior) in shared control human-autonomy teams. We considered how collaboration strategies change based on the instructions that the person was provided and the relative weighing of the shared control between the human and the autonomy. We further sought to consider how the autonomy can learn how to adjust the sharing of control between the human and the autonomy over repeated iterations.
