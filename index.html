Aleksandra Dudek

I am a 4th year graduate student in the Department of Mechanical Engineering at the University of Michigan. I am co-advised by Kira Barton in the Barton Research Group and Chris Vermillion in the Control and Optimization for Renewables and Energy Efficiency (CORE) lab.

My goal is to develop assistive autonomous systems that adapt their support in real time to the person that they are working with while balancing energetic costs and time-varying dynamics.

Adaptive Driver Training for Semi-Autonomous Vehicles


abstract

I developed a paradigm to better prepare drivers to take over an autonomous vehicle in the presence of an obstacle that the autonomy fails to perceive. We iterated on various ways to estimate model parameters online. In a user study, adaptive training produced a 20% reduction in take-over variability between users compared to the baseline protocol. This work has been submitted to IEEE Transactions on Human-Machine Systems.

Game-Theoretic Modeling of Human-Autonomy Teaming


instructions / control weighting / adaptive arbitration

My initial work sought to develop a theoretically grounded method to characterize human behavior (i.e., cognitive, high-level behavior) in shared control human-autonomy teams. We considered how collaboration strategies change based on the instructions that the person was provided and the relative weighting of the shared control between the human and the autonomy. We further studied how the autonomy can learn to adjust the sharing of control between the human and the autonomy over repeated iterations.
